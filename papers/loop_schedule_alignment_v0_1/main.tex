\begin{abstract}
Repeated use of one parameterized block changes how its update is accumulated
and read through an unrolled computation. DeepLoop formalizes this effect with
a visit-alignment coefficient $\kappa_R$ and analyzes decorrelated and aligned
envelopes, but leaves direct measurement of the coefficient as future
work~\cite{li2026deeploop}. We directly estimate visit alignment in small tied
residual loops from per-visit suffix sensitivities and single-live-application
parameter gradients. An initial three-point measurement at
$R\in\{2,4,8\}$ gave $\kappa\propto R^{0.423}$ with $R^2=0.994$. A
preregistered extension finds that alignment grows during training, from
$\gamma=0.130$ at initialization to $0.309$ after 4,096 state-visit exposures.
A held-out $R=16$ point falls below the three-point extrapolation, reducing the
terminal four-point exponent to $0.309$ with 95\% seed-bootstrap interval
$[0.287,0.328]$. A mini causal attention+MLP loop yields tied
$\gamma=0.501$ versus untied $\gamma=0.018$, although its strict composite
replication gate fails because the flat untied sequence has poor power-law fit.
All 96 cells in a residual-scaling learning-rate ladder remain stable, leaving
the boundary left-censored at $p\le 0$. These small-scale results reject the
fully aligned envelope as a descriptive model in this regime, not as a
conservative bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

A looped model applies a physical block repeatedly without increasing stored
parameters. DeepLoop identifies the resulting tied-depth term: one shared
update aggregates contributions from repeated visits and is then read through
those visits in the next linearized forward pass~\cite{li2026deeploop}. Its
analysis is controlled by $\kappa_R$, with decorrelated and fully aligned
envelopes. The paper explicitly identifies direct measurement of $\kappa_R$ or
cross-round gradient alignment as future work.

We ask three narrow questions. Does a direct estimator distinguish tied from
untied visits? Does the estimated exponent transfer to a held-out loop count?
Is alignment fixed by architecture, or does it change during training? We also
test one small attention+MLP block and revisit the associated stability
boundary. We make no claim about language-model accuracy or large-scale
transfer.

\section{Estimator and Schedule Correction}

For each gradient-visible visit $r$, let $U_r$ be a top-direction Jacobian
vector product through the schedule suffix after that visit. Let $G_r$ be the
parameter gradient from a recomputed loss in which only visit $r$ applies its
module with live parameters; all other applications use detached parameters.
We measure
\begin{equation}
\kappa_g =
\frac{\left\|\sum_r U_r\right\|\left\|\sum_r G_r\right\|}
{R_g\max_r\|U_r\|\max_r\|G_r\|},
\qquad 0\le\kappa_g\le R_g.
\end{equation}
Residual scale is excluded from $\kappa_g$ and applied once in the schedule
functional
\begin{equation}
B(A)=\sum_j J_jR_g(j)\kappa_g(j)
\left(\frac{\beta_j}{\alpha_j}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Here $R_g(j)$ counts gradient-visible applications rather than all forward
visits. This distinction is required for truncated or masked schedules; using
$R(j)$ silently restores contributions that autograd cannot transmit.

We fit $\log\kappa_R=c+\gamma\log R$ to geometric-mean kappas across three
seeds. The estimator uses five power iterations and one fixed probe direction
per seed at all checkpoints.

\section{Registered Design}

The primary model is a hidden-size-128 two-linear-layer residual loop on
deterministic signed-permutation regression. Tied and visit-untied schedules
use $R\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, seeds $\{101,103,107\}$, and 4,096 state-visit
exposures. Tied kappa is measured at exposures
$\{0,512,1024,2048,4096\}$. The v0 three-point fit is frozen before observing
$R=16$:
\begin{equation}
\log\widehat\kappa_R=0.025971+0.422909\log R,
\qquad \widehat\kappa_{16}=3.315221.
\end{equation}
A holdout ratio in $[0.8,1.25]$ confirms extrapolation.

The external-validity cell replaces the block and task with a hidden-size-64
manual four-head causal attention+MLP residual block on deterministic
prefix-context regression. The strict gate requires both tied and untied fits
to have $R^2\ge0.8$ and a gamma contrast of at least 0.1.

The stability ladder crosses $R\in\{6,12\}$,
$p\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.15\}$, learning rates
$\{0.0003,0.001,0.003,0.01\}$, and three seeds. A run is stable if finite,
with maximum gradient norm at most 100 and final-to-initial loss ratio at most
10. A cell uses two-of-three seed majority. Grid edges remain censored.

Gamma rises by 0.1790 and all four transitions are nondecreasing, satisfying
the frozen \texttt{learned\_growth} rule. Early $R^2$ values are weak. Thus a
power-law relation does not simply retain its form while changing exponent;
the relation itself becomes better formed over training.

The fixed-direction terminal probes at $R=2,4,8$ exactly reproduce the sealed
v0 kappas. The held-out tied value is $\kappa_{16}=2.550934$, a ratio of
0.769461 to the frozen prediction and a log residual of $-0.262065$. The
three-point extrapolation is therefore rejected. Refitting all four points
gives $\gamma=0.309485$ and $R^2=0.914049$. The untied point estimate remains
near zero at $0.017527$, although its $R^2=0.796134$ narrowly misses the
registered fit-quality threshold.

\section{Attention+MLP Replication}

\caption{Terminal kappa scaling for the primary residual MLP and the mini
attention+MLP cell. Weight tying produces a positive exponent in both. The

\label{fig:scaling}

The mini attention+MLP loop gives tied $\gamma=0.501246$ with
$R^2=0.854021$. Its untied point estimate is $0.017859$, but
$R^2=0.050919$ because the four kappas form a noisy flat sequence rather than
a power law. The gamma contrast is $0.483387$.

The strict composite replication gate is not confirmed because it required a
high-quality untied power-law fit. That condition is poorly matched to a flat
negative control, but changing it after outcomes would conflate preregistered
confirmation with post-hoc interpretation. The licensed positive result is
therefore mechanistic: the tied coefficient scales on a second small model and
task while the untied point estimate remains near zero.

\section{The Stability Boundary Still Does Not Bind}

All 96 seed-level ladder runs are stable. The maximum observed gradient norm is
66.0719 and the maximum final-to-initial loss ratio is 0.6855. Every aggregate
boundary is left-censored at $p\le0$ for both loop counts and all learning
rates. The learning-rate ladder therefore does not rescue the proposed
threshold as an ordering in this regime.

\section{Discussion}

The direct measurement supports four conclusions. First, weight tying creates
measurable visit alignment: both tied model families have positive exponents,
whereas untied point estimates are near zero. Second, alignment is partly
learned rather than purely architectural. It is positive at initialization but
grows substantially over 4,096 exposures. Third, the original
$\gamma=0.423$ three-point estimate was not a transferable constant; $R=16$
supplies the residual check that the initial fit lacked. Fourth, the fully
aligned $\gamma=1$ case is conservative rather than descriptive here: the
primary terminal upper interval is 0.328.

This does not falsify DeepLoop's sufficient conservative parameterization.
Instead, it rejects interpreting the aligned envelope as the measured behavior
of these small loops. To our knowledge, this is the first direct estimate of
DeepLoop's specific visit-alignment coefficient, but that novelty statement is
scoped to our recorded literature audit rather than an exhaustive survey.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility}

The primary network is small and synthetic. The attention+MLP cell changes
model and task together and is not a language model. Three seeds provide only
a small-sample interval. The short-horizon stability rule may be permissive,
and every ladder boundary remains censored. No task-accuracy, sample-efficiency,
large-scale Transformer, or general stability claim follows.
